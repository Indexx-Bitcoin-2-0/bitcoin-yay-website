Brian, I recommend building BTCY around a useful rewards economy with transparent accounting, then adding a separately structured investment offering if you want to raise investor capital. Increasing “mining power” alone may attract initial purchases, but lasting interest requires people to understand what they receive, where rewards come from, and how they can use them.

I’m treating Bitcoin Y as Bitcoin YAY (BTCY). This is a proposed structure; the current power-mining contracts, financial results, and software would need review before implementation.

1. Start with the business model

First, decide which product you are offering:

Model	What people receive	What the system must demonstrate
Rewards membership	Useful services, discounts, and activity rewards	Benefits worth paying for independently of token appreciation
Actual mining service	A defined share of real computing capacity and mining output	Equipment, measurable hash rate, operating expenses, and verifiable payouts
Investment offering	Ownership or contractual rights to financial returns	A legally structured offering, disclosures, accounting, and investor eligibility

My recommendation is a rewards membership for customers, alongside a separate company investment route for investors. Separation improves clarity, but it does not automatically resolve securities requirements.

If BTCY rewards are allocated by your servers rather than earned through blockchain consensus, describe that mechanism accurately. Calling it “mining” does not give it the regulatory treatment of Bitcoin mining.

Current SEC guidance explains that even a non-security crypto asset can be offered through an investment contract when the arrangement includes promises of managerial efforts and satisfies the other Howey elements. Counsel should assess the complete BTCY arrangement before you market paid power packages for financial returns. 

2. Build this overall structure

Layer	Purpose	What to build
Customer experience	Help people understand and use BTCY	Simple onboarding, benefits, rewards history, spending, support
Revenue engine	Generate money from outside investor deposits	Merchant campaigns, sponsorships, advertising, useful subscriptions
Rewards engine	Allocate rewards consistently	Published rules, budgets, eligibility, fraud controls
Financial foundation	Account for every balance and obligation	Separate ledgers for cash, tokens, pending rewards, and withdrawals
Trust and control	Make the program credible	Operating reports, security, complaint handling, approval controls

The central business loop should be:

Merchants pay for measurable customer activity → customers receive useful benefits → successful merchants purchase more campaigns.

Selling power packages should not become the funding source for paying earlier participants’ promised returns.

3. Give people reasons to participate beyond price speculation

I would prioritize three features:

* BTCY merchant offers: Real discounts or rewards for purchases from participating businesses.
* Member benefits: Services people would buy even if BTCY’s market price stayed unchanged—for example, useful shopping or AI tools, provided you can deliver them economically.
* A transparent rewards dashboard: Show what was earned, why it was earned, whether it is transferable, and what restrictions apply.

For any paid membership, list the actual services and limits. Avoid selling a higher reward multiplier as though it is a predictable income stream. Removing the word “investment” does not change the economics.

Your marketing should demonstrate completed purchases, useful benefits, and reliable service. Avoid guaranteed returns, invented earnings examples, or testimonials that imply typical profits without evidence. The FTC specifically identifies guaranteed-return claims and misleading crypto-mining investment tiers as common warning signs. 

4. Do you need an app?

You need a good mobile experience, but you do not need to build another native app immediately.

Start with a mobile-friendly website or progressive web app. If the existing BTCY app is reliable, add these features there using the same backend.

Build first	Add when usage justifies it
Account creation and secure login	More native phone features
Clear explanation of BTCY and membership	Advanced personalization
Rewards and transaction history	Wider merchant integrations
Merchant redemption	Additional blockchain networks
Support and administration	More complex community features

Keep Yates integration optional initially. Users should be able to understand and use BTCY without completing several separate app registrations.

5. Technology your developers need

Use a straightforward architecture:

Component	Requirement
Mobile interface	Responsive web interface connected to a shared backend
Backend	Accounts, memberships, campaigns, rewards, and redemption APIs
Financial database	Transactional database with a double-entry ledger and audit history
Rewards processor	Versioned rules, scheduled calculations, spending caps, duplicate prevention
Blockchain connection	One supported network initially, transaction monitoring, reconciliation
Wallet security	Clear custody model, protected signing keys, withdrawal controls, recovery procedures
Administration	Role-based access, dual approval for sensitive actions, support tools
Analytics	Acquisition, retention, merchant results, revenue, reward costs, withdrawal performance

Use AI for support, content drafts, and detecting suspicious patterns. Keep balances, reward calculations, and payment approvals under deterministic software rules and human controls.

Blockchain can record transfers. It does not establish profitability, guarantee liquidity, or make an offering compliant.

6. Make the economics work before promoting growth

Your finance team should calculate:

Collected external revenue − operating costs − refunds − fraud losses − required reserves = amount potentially available for rewards.

Not all of that amount should necessarily be distributed.

Track token obligations separately from cash expenses. Issuing your own tokens is not the same as generating cash revenue, and displaying a token value does not prove users can sell at that price.

Test at least three conditions:

* New member purchases stop for three months.
* Advertising and merchant revenue fall by 50%.
* Withdrawal requests rise sharply.

If the program cannot meet its contractual obligations under those conditions, revise the model before selling more packages.

7. A practical 90-day development plan

Period	Work	Completion requirement
Days 1–15	Review existing power packages, reward sources, withdrawal obligations, user complaints, and legal classification	Written product definition and complete money-flow map
Days 16–30	Design the customer journey, ledger, reward rules, and merchant pilot	Working prototype and reviewed economics
Days 31–60	Build onboarding, dashboard, redemption, administration, and reporting	Transactions reconcile; security and withdrawal workflows pass testing
Days 61–90	Pilot with approximately 100–300 users and a small merchant group	Evidence of repeat use, merchant value, manageable support costs, and reliable fulfillment

Treat those pilot numbers as planning assumptions, not forecasts. Expand only after the pilot demonstrates value.

8. How to build interest

Begin with existing BTCY members. Interview active users, inactive users, and people who purchased power mining. Find out whether the main issue is confusing benefits, weak utility, withdrawals, trust, or onboarding.

Then:

1. Publish a plain-language “How BTCY works” explanation.
2. Demonstrate one useful merchant redemption from start to finish.
3. Report verified operating results monthly.
4. Reward genuine customer activity rather than recruitment spending.
5. Scale marketing only after repeat use and sustainable economics appear.

If you raise company investment, prepare that offering separately with counsel. Do not launch investment-linked referral commissions without legal review.

Your first instruction to the team should be: “Map every dollar and every BTCY reward—from its source to its final use—and build one customer benefit people will return to use.” That foundation will make BTCY easier to market and give prospective investors something concrete to evaluate.
