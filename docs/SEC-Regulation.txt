SEC Regulations for BTCY Power Mining and Investment Returns

U.S. Securities Law | October 2026

For your Bitcoin Yay (BTCY) project, the most important point is that the SEC does not establish a universal maximum investment return of 10%, 15%, or 20% annually.

The primary legal issue is not how high the return is. It is how BTCY obtains investors’ money, how the returns are generated, what BTCY promises, and whether the arrangement constitutes a security.

For example, if a customer pays $10,000 for Power Mining and BTCY promises a 15% annual return in BTCY Nuggets, the arrangement may be considered an investment contract subject to U.S. securities laws.

1. Five major regulations to understand

Regulation	Why it matters for BTCY
Securities Act of 1933	Securities offerings generally must be registered or qualify for an exemption.
Howey investment-contract test	Determines whether the economic arrangement is an investment contract.
Regulation D	Provides exemptions for certain private securities offerings, including Rules 506(b) and 506(c).
Regulation Crowdfunding	Allows qualifying companies to raise capital through registered funding intermediaries, subject to limits and disclosures.
Securities Exchange Act of 1934	Includes antifraud requirements and may create additional registration or intermediary obligations.

There are also potential state securities, consumer protection, money transmission, commodities, and tax requirements.

2. How the SEC would evaluate BTCY

The SEC’s March 2026 crypto-asset interpretation provides updated guidance. Importantly, even when a crypto asset itself is not a security, its sale can involve an investment contract because of promises made by the issuer.

Consider these BTCY structures:

BTCY activity	Securities risk
Free Nuggets earned through ordinary app activity	Generally lower, depending on the arrangement
Genuine proof-of-work mining performed by users	Potentially lower under SEC mining guidance
Selling a real mining service without promised returns	Fact-dependent
Selling $10,000 Power Mining packages advertised to earn 15% annually	Significant
Accepting $10,000 and promising $11,500 after one year	Significant; may also be a note or debt security

The SEC has issued guidance distinguishing certain genuine proof-of-work mining activities from securities offerings. That does not automatically cover app-generated, off-chain BTCY Nuggets or packages promising returns from company-managed activities.

3. Can BTCY legally offer 15% annual returns?

Potentially yes, but the offering must be properly structured. A high return does not by itself make a product illegal or exempt it from securities regulation.

Here are the principal pathways to evaluate:

Structure	Potential use
Regulation D, Rule 506(b)	Private capital raising without public solicitation; accredited investors and up to 35 qualifying non-accredited investors within a 90-day period, subject to conditions
Regulation D, Rule 506(c)	Publicly advertised securities offering, but all purchasers must be accredited and their status verified
Regulation Crowdfunding	Public investment opportunity through a registered intermediary, with offering and investor limits
Non-investment mining service	Genuine delivery of mining-related services without representing the purchase as an investment

Rule 506(b) and 506(c) can permit unlimited capital raises, but each has different marketing and investor qualification requirements.

Very important for BTCY marketing: If you advertise a 15% investment return on your public website, YouTube, or social media, that may constitute general solicitation. This could disqualify a proposed Rule 506(b) private offering.

4. What I would specifically avoid

* Promising fixed 10%–15% returns without a compliant offering and credible means of payment.
* Describing BTCY Nuggets as equivalent to cash or interest when they lack assured conversion and liquidity.
* Using new purchasers’ payments to finance returns to earlier purchasers.
* Advertising an investment to the general public before choosing an appropriate registration or exemption pathway.
* Paying unregistered referral agents transaction-based commissions for selling securities without legal review.

Also, changing terminology from investment to mining, reward, or membership does not determine the regulatory outcome.

5. My recommended structure for Bitcoin Yay

I would evaluate two separate products.

BTCY Power Mining — Consumer Product

A transparent, genuinely delivered mining or computing service. Purchasers receive specified services, not a guaranteed annual financial return. Any Nugget rewards must be described according to how they are actually generated.

BTCY Investment Program — Securities Offering

A separately documented offering, potentially under Regulation D, with investor qualification, financial disclosures, legal agreements, risk disclosures, and applicable SEC and state filings. A 10%–15% target could be evaluated, but not advertised as guaranteed unless the obligations and risks are properly established.

Before launching either program, I recommend obtaining a written analysis from a U.S. securities attorney familiar with crypto mining, token rewards, and digital-asset investment contracts.
